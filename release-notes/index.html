Bodhrán Release Notes

What has changed in the bodhrán accompaniment app since version 1.1.0. The version number shows next to the title in the app, so you can check which one your phone or browser is running.

  • 1.1.0 → 1.6.0 over three days, 26–28 September 2026
  • 36 → 71 automated checks
New feature Fixes and smaller changes
1.6.028 Sep

Guitar backing demonstration

  • A separate page at /guitar/, apart from the app: The Kesh, from setting 1 on thesession.org, with a synthesised guitar backing.
  • Turn the guitar, the tune (a flute) and the bodhrán on and off, choose a lilting or a driving strum, and set the tempo and how many times through.
  • The chord chart lights the bar being played, and each chord’s shape is drawn, in drone voicings that keep D and G ringing on the top strings.
1.5.228 Sep

These release notes

  • This page, on the app’s own site and linked from What’s new in the About.
  • Offline, every page on the site now comes from its own saved copy. Before, any page other than the app itself came up as a broken copy of the app.
1.5.128 Sep

Waltz length

  • Waltzes now start at 64 bars, since their parts are usually 16 bars long. Every other tune type starts at 32.
1.5.028 Sep

Play it N times, then finish

  • New Length (16, 32, 48 or 64 bars) and Play it (until I stop, or 1 to 6 times) settings under the bar display. Each tune type remembers its own length.
  • On the last time through, the drum lands one final stroke on beat 1 of the tune’s last bar, with your final note, and stops.
  • The counter follows the tune, for example “bar 13 of 32 · time 2 of 3”.
  • Lowering the number part-way through ends at the end of the time you’re on.
1.4.028 Sep

Basic and Advanced controls

  • A Basic / Advanced switch under the title. Basic is the default and shows the everyday controls: tempo, tune type, rhythm, the bar display, count-in, volume and the drone.
  • In Basic, each panel lists what it keeps back (“More in Advanced: tuning, tone, back hand, room”), and tapping that line opens Advanced right there.
  • Hidden settings still work, and Basic flags any that have been changed from where they started.
1.3.328 Sep

Mac Safari silence fixed

  • After the Mac slept, a Safari tab could go silent while looking as if it was playing, however often it was reloaded. Pressing Play now wakes the sound.
  • Added a sound test page, /tests/sound.html, for tracking down silent playback.
1.3.228 Sep

iPhone setting kept to the iPhone

  • The setting that lets an iPhone play with its silent switch on no longer runs on the Mac, where it has no job.
1.3.127 Sep

B for the drone, and small fixes

  • Added B to the drone, for tunes in B minor.
  • Typing a tempo outside a tune type’s range now shows the range, instead of changing it without a word.
  • Arrow keys move between tune types and between rhythms on the Mac.
  • Count-in clicks are sent to GarageBand, on their own note (side stick, 37), so they never sound like drum strokes.
  • Changing tune mid-bar no longer resets your swing until the next bar.
1.3.027 Sep

Back hand

  • New Back hand control, off by default: the player’s other hand pressing the skin from inside. It raises the pitch and shortens the ring, following one arc across each phrase, so the fill falls in pitch into the next one.
1.2.027 Sep

Finish

  • New Finish button, or the F key, plays to the end of the phrase and ends with one stroke on beat 1 of the last bar. Press it again to carry on.
  • Fixed: the count-in bar used to count as a bar of the tune, so every fill landed a bar early, on bars 3 and 7.
1.1.127 Sep

Fixes and more patterns

  • Fixed the slide’s only fill, which played a whole bar squashed into two beats.
  • Added fills and busy bars for slides, barndances, hornpipes and slip jigs.
  • Stop now silences strokes that were already queued.
  • Changing tune waits for the next bar, and no longer plays the old tune’s pattern under the new one.
  • The About text matches the current layout, and the GarageBand panel is one line on iPhone.

Guitar demo

The guitar backing page at /guitar/ has its own version number, shown next to its title.

Guitar demo 1.3.128 Sep

The bodhrán brought forward

  • The bodhrán was buried under the bassier guitar: 4 dB under it overall, and its stick click, the part your ear picks a drum out by, far under the guitar. It now sits level with the guitar, with the click lifted so it cuts through.
  • The count-in clicks stay as loud as before, and the drum in the bodhrán app itself is unchanged.
Guitar demo 1.3.028 Sep

DADGAD tuning

  • The guitar now starts in DADGAD (D A D G A D), with a switch back to standard tuning to compare. The new tuning comes in at the next bar, so you can switch while it plays.
  • The DADGAD shapes leave strings open: the top D rings through every chord and the open G through three. G and C carry an added ninth, D is an open fifth, and Em is Em7.
  • The shape diagrams follow the tuning, with each string’s note under it and the ringing strings highlighted.
Guitar demo 1.2.028 Sep

A volume for each sound

  • A slider for the guitar, the flute and the bodhrán, beside each one’s on/off tick. 100% is the measured balance, with the guitar leading; each goes up to 200%.
  • Changes are heard straight away, even mid-tune, and the page remembers them.
Guitar demo 1.1.028 Sep

A bigger, bassier guitar

  • More like a Martin dreadnought: a deeper body resonance and a fuller low end, the mids scooped a little, a softer pick on the bass strings, and bass notes that ring on. Measured, the bass came up by 9 dB against the rest.
  • The demo’s own version number, next to its title and at the foot of its page.
Guitar demo 1.0.028 Sep

The first version

  • The Kesh with a guitar backing, released alongside app 1.6.0.

The musical changes (the back hand, the new fills, how Finish ends) follow bodhrán technique rather than drum-kit habits: down strokes on the beat, up strokes between, and the weight where the dance puts it. The final say on how they sound is by ear.

Every change came with automated checks that run against the real app, and each new check was shown to catch the fault it guards against.
