Bodhrán Release Notes

What has changed in the bodhrán accompaniment app since version 1.1.0. The version number shows next to the title in the app, so you can check which one your phone or browser is running.

  • 1.1.0 → 1.7.1 over five days, 26–30 September 2026
  • 36 → 71 automated checks
New feature Fixes and smaller changes
1.7.130 Sep

Switch to Session Players

  • The foot of the page now switches between the app and Session Players.
1.7.030 Sep

A longer count-in

  • The count-in now starts at two bars, time to start the drum and pick up your instrument. A new four-bar choice gives more.
  • The bar counter counts the lead-in (“count-in 1 of 2”), so you can see when the drum comes in.
  • A count-in you had already chosen yourself is kept.
1.6.629 Sep

Safari’s other way to the speaker

  • In Safari on a Mac, the app’s sound now goes to the speaker the way a song or video plays, instead of straight out. When a Safari tab went silent, the sound check said the app was making sound: Safari was losing it. In that tab, sound sent this other way still played, and brought the direct way back.
  • Chrome and the iPhone are unchanged.
1.6.529 Sep

Sound check

  • While it plays, a line under Play says whether the app itself is making sound, and how loud. If it says it is and you hear nothing, the sound is being lost in Safari, not in the app.
1.6.429 Sep

A stronger Safari wake-up

  • In Safari on a Mac, each Play now sends a second of sound too quiet to hear, in place of a twentieth of a second of silence. A plain one-second audio file is what got a silent tab going again; the old wake-up didn’t.
  • The sound test page has a new button, F, for trying just the wake-up, and says what to press when the app has gone silent.
1.6.329 Sep

Lets go of the speaker when stopped

  • Stopped, with the drone off, the app lets go of the speaker a few seconds after the last stroke, and takes it back on the next Play. Before, a stopped tab kept its audio open, and the Mac from sleeping on its own, for as long as it stayed open.
  • Each Play now starts Safari’s sound afresh. It didn’t cure the silent tab (see 1.6.4).
1.6.228 Sep

No stumble in a background tab

  • In Safari, a tab left playing behind another tab in the same window used to stumble: Safari slows a background tab’s timers to about once a second, even while it plays. The drum now plans several seconds ahead the moment its tab is hidden. Stop still stops at once.
  • The bar display and counter show the bar being heard, so they don’t run ahead of the music when you come back to the tab.
1.6.128 Sep

One fix, shared

  • The fix for Mac Safari going silent after sleep now lives in one place, shared with the guitar demo, so the two can’t drift apart. The app sounds just the same.
1.6.028 Sep

Guitar backing demonstration

  • A separate page at /guitar/, apart from the app: The Kesh, from setting 1 on thesession.org, with a synthesised guitar backing.
  • Turn the guitar, the tune (a flute) and the bodhrán on and off, choose a lilting or a driving strum, and set the tempo and how many times through.
  • The chord chart lights the bar being played, and each chord’s shape is drawn, in drone voicings that keep D and G ringing on the top strings.
1.5.228 Sep

These release notes

  • This page, on the app’s own site and linked from What’s new in the About.
  • Offline, every page on the site now comes from its own saved copy. Before, any page other than the app itself came up as a broken copy of the app.
1.5.128 Sep

Waltz length

  • Waltzes now start at 64 bars, since their parts are usually 16 bars long. Every other tune type starts at 32.
1.5.028 Sep

Play it N times, then finish

  • New Length (16, 32, 48 or 64 bars) and Play it (until I stop, or 1 to 6 times) settings under the bar display. Each tune type remembers its own length.
  • On the last time through, the drum lands one final stroke on beat 1 of the tune’s last bar, with your final note, and stops.
  • The counter follows the tune, for example “bar 13 of 32 · time 2 of 3”.
  • Lowering the number part-way through ends at the end of the time you’re on.
1.4.028 Sep

Basic and Advanced controls

  • A Basic / Advanced switch under the title. Basic is the default and shows the everyday controls: tempo, tune type, rhythm, the bar display, count-in, volume and the drone.
  • In Basic, each panel lists what it keeps back (“More in Advanced: tuning, tone, back hand, room”), and tapping that line opens Advanced right there.
  • Hidden settings still work, and Basic flags any that have been changed from where they started.
1.3.328 Sep

Mac Safari silence fixed

  • After the Mac slept, a Safari tab could go silent while looking as if it was playing, however often it was reloaded. Pressing Play now wakes the sound.
  • Added a sound test page, /tests/sound.html, for tracking down silent playback.
1.3.228 Sep

iPhone setting kept to the iPhone

  • The setting that lets an iPhone play with its silent switch on no longer runs on the Mac, where it has no job.
1.3.127 Sep

B for the drone, and small fixes

  • Added B to the drone, for tunes in B minor.
  • Typing a tempo outside a tune type’s range now shows the range, instead of changing it without a word.
  • Arrow keys move between tune types and between rhythms on the Mac.
  • Count-in clicks are sent to GarageBand, on their own note (side stick, 37), so they never sound like drum strokes.
  • Changing tune mid-bar no longer resets your swing until the next bar.
1.3.027 Sep

Back hand

  • New Back hand control, off by default: the player’s other hand pressing the skin from inside. It raises the pitch and shortens the ring, following one arc across each phrase, so the fill falls in pitch into the next one.
1.2.027 Sep

Finish

  • New Finish button, or the F key, plays to the end of the phrase and ends with one stroke on beat 1 of the last bar. Press it again to carry on.
  • Fixed: the count-in bar used to count as a bar of the tune, so every fill landed a bar early, on bars 3 and 7.
1.1.127 Sep

Fixes and more patterns

  • Fixed the slide’s only fill, which played a whole bar squashed into two beats.
  • Added fills and busy bars for slides, barndances, hornpipes and slip jigs.
  • Stop now silences strokes that were already queued.
  • Changing tune waits for the next bar, and no longer plays the old tune’s pattern under the new one.
  • The About text matches the current layout, and the GarageBand panel is one line on iPhone.

Guitar demo 1.9.129 Sep

Lets go of the speaker when stopped

  • Stopped, the demo lets go of the speaker once the last notes ring out, and takes it back on the next Play, the same as the app.
Guitar demo 1.9.029 Sep

DADGAD drones on D

  • In DADGAD, the open low D and top D now drone under every chord: G, C and Em are played over the open low D, the way DADGAD backing is known for. Before, G and C started on their own roots as in standard tuning, which is why the two tunings sounded alike.
  • The low D now sounds in every bar, 5 to 11 dB under the whole guitar; before, it was 35 to 44 dB down in the G and C bars. The chord chart is unchanged.
  • The “ring through changes” switch is gone: it was heard in only two bars, and it thinned the sound where a note was struck again. “Ring in sympathy” stays.
Guitar demo 1.8.128 Sep

A drone you can hear

  • Ringing through was barely audible: the open low D only faded under the C. Left unmuted, it is now struck along with the new chord, so in DADGAD the low D drones through the C in bars 3 and 7 of the A part.
Guitar demo 1.8.028 Sep

Open strings ring through chord changes

  • A string the next chord leaves out is only stopped if it was fretted; ringing open, it carries on under the new chord. In DADGAD, as D goes to C in the A part, the open low D now rings on under the C.
  • An open string struck again carries on into the new stroke instead of being cut off. A switch turns both off and on, to compare.
Guitar demo 1.7.028 Sep

Open strings ring in sympathy

  • On a real guitar the open strings hum along with the notes they share, which is much of what makes DADGAD sound like DADGAD. The demo’s guitar now does this, with a switch to compare.
  • Neither tuning is favoured: each open string rings as much as it is in tune with the others. DADGAD, with more strings open, all D, A and G, rings noticeably more, most of all on its open-fifth D.
Guitar demo 1.6.028 Sep

Bass runs off, for now

  • The bass runs are taken off the page: no runs, no switch, no marks in the chart. The work behind them is kept, so they can come back.
Guitar demo 1.5.028 Sep

Thumb-plucked bass runs

  • The run notes are rebuilt as a low string plucked with the thumb. They used to start from a burst of noise, which made them sound synthesised; now each starts from the bend a thumb puts in the string, so its tone is clean and round.
  • The string vibrates two ways at once for a natural shimmer and long tail, its pitch settles the way a plucked string’s does, and the guitar’s body rings along with it.
Guitar demo 1.4.228 Sep

Heavier, darker bass runs

  • The runs still sounded bright and a bit weak. Their notes now come from a string that loses its top as it rings, the way a real one does, with a rounder attack, more thumb than pick tip.
  • They sit a few dB above the strums, as a bass line should, instead of level with them.
Guitar demo 1.4.128 Sep

Bass runs that sound like a guitar

  • The runs sounded like a piano: picked alone, their notes stayed as bright half a second in as at the start, the way a piano’s do. A guitar string starts bright and settles warm.
  • The run notes now have their own voice: a firm pick near the bridge that darkens as it rings, a knock from the guitar’s top, more low end, and more level, so they sit with the strums instead of under them.
Guitar demo 1.4.028 Sep

Bass runs between chords

  • On the last beat before a chord change, three single bass notes walk up into the new chord: G A B into C, D E F♯ into G, A B C♯ into D. They lead into bars 2 and 5 of each part.
  • A switch turns them off and on (on to begin with), the chord chart marks the bars that have one, and the Now panel names each run as it plays.
  • Each run sits where the tuning plays it: in DADGAD the walk into G starts on the open low D; in standard tuning it climbs the D string to the open G.
Guitar demo 1.3.328 Sep

  • Like the app, the demo no longer stumbles in Safari when left playing behind another tab, and Stop drops every note already queued.
Guitar demo 1.3.228 Sep

  • After the Mac slept, the demo could go silent in Safari while looking as if it played, as the app once did. It now wakes the sound the same way, from the same code as the app.
Guitar demo 1.3.128 Sep

The bodhrán brought forward

  • The bodhrán was buried under the bassier guitar: 4 dB under it overall, and its stick click, the part your ear picks a drum out by, far under the guitar. It now sits level with the guitar, with the click lifted so it cuts through.
  • The count-in clicks stay as loud as before, and the drum in the bodhrán app itself is unchanged.
Guitar demo 1.3.028 Sep

DADGAD tuning

  • The guitar now starts in DADGAD (D A D G A D), with a switch back to standard tuning to compare. The new tuning comes in at the next bar, so you can switch while it plays.
  • The DADGAD shapes leave strings open: the top D rings through every chord and the open G through three. G and C carry an added ninth, D is an open fifth, and Em is Em7.
  • The shape diagrams follow the tuning, with each string’s note under it and the ringing strings highlighted.
Guitar demo 1.2.028 Sep

A volume for each sound

  • A slider for the guitar, the flute and the bodhrán, beside each one’s on/off tick. 100% is the measured balance, with the guitar leading; each goes up to 200%.
  • Changes are heard straight away, even mid-tune, and the page remembers them.
Guitar demo 1.1.028 Sep

A bigger, bassier guitar

  • More like a Martin dreadnought: a deeper body resonance and a fuller low end, the mids scooped a little, a softer pick on the bass strings, and bass notes that ring on. Measured, the bass came up by 9 dB against the rest.

Session Players 1.0.030 Sep

Find a tune, and play along

  • Name a tune and it is found on thesession.org and given a backing: guitar, bodhrán, and the melody on the flute. Untick the flute to play it yourself. Jigs and reels for now.
  • Choose any of the tune’s settings; repeats, endings and pickups are played as written.
  • Chords are chosen from the melody (or taken from the setting, if it has them). Tap any bar to change its chord; the change holds every time that bar comes round.
  • Save the tune to a file, with your chords, and open it again later with nothing fetched.

The musical changes (the back hand, the new fills, how Finish ends) follow bodhrán technique rather than drum-kit habits: down strokes on the beat, up strokes between, and the weight where the dance puts it. The final say on how they sound is by ear.

Every change came with automated checks that run against the real app, and each new check was shown to catch the fault it guards against.
